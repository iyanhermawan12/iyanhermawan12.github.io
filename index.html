ScamLens slows that moment down. It gives you a second opinion, highlights the parts that look risky, and helps you decide whether to trust, ignore, report, or verify the message through another channel.

✨ Key Features

ScamLens combines several checks in one place so you do not need to understand the technical details behind them.

  • Message analysis that reviews the text for pressure tactics, unusual requests, and known scam patterns.
  • Screenshot analysis that reads text from an image so you can check a chat, email, or social post.
  • URL inspection that looks at a link for signs of phishing or misleading web addresses.
  • Plain-language explanations that describe why something may be risky.
  • Risk indicators that point to urgency, payment requests, credential requests, impersonation, and social engineering.
  • Safe action recommendations that suggest the next step, such as verifying through an official app or avoiding the link.
  • A clear risk summary that helps you compare the warning signs in one view.

🧭 How ScamLens Helps You Decide

A scam warning is more useful when it tells you what to look at. ScamLens separates the content into simple parts: what was said, what link was included, what action was requested, and what risk signals appeared. That structure makes it easier to pause and check the message before you respond.

For example, if a message says your account will be closed unless you confirm your password today, ScamLens can point out the urgency, the password request, and the link. It can explain that a real company usually does not ask for your password through a message link. The recommendation may be to open the company website or app yourself instead of using the link in the message.

The app is built for clarity. It does not expect you to read code, understand machine learning, or know security terms. It focuses on the practical question: does this look safe, and what should I do next?

🖥️ Using ScamLens on Windows

When you open ScamLens on your Windows computer, you are greeted with a simple starting area for the content you want to check. You can choose the type of item you have, such as a message, a screenshot, or a link. Then you add the content and ask ScamLens to review it.

For a message, copy the text into the message box. For a screenshot, select the image from your computer. For a link, paste the web address into the link field. ScamLens reviews the content and shows the result in a way that is meant to be easy to read.

You can use ScamLens on your own for a quick check, or you can use it with a friend or family member when a message feels confusing. The app keeps the focus on explanation, so you can see the warning signs instead of guessing.
